Put a question to several models and get an answer you can audit.

Polyvise runs a question through a structured format — adversarial debate, consensus finding, or an advisory panel — using several independent models at once. What comes back is a verdict where every claim links to the source behind it and every step names the model that produced it.

Engine pipeline

What happens after you start a run

01
Frame
Turns your question into a resolution the agents can argue for or against, and classifies the topic and its stakes.
02
Scout
Agents stake out positions and list their assumptions independently, before any of them see each other's work.
03
Evidence
Retrieves sources, grades each one primary, expert or methodology, and screens out anything promotional or undated.
04
Deliberate
Opening, cross-examination, rebuttal and closing rounds. Each turn records which claims and sources it rests on.
05
Judge
A neutral model scores five dimensions, writes the verdict, and lists the evidence that would change it.
Principle 01

Failed steps are reported, not filled in

When a model fails, the affected section stays empty and reports which model was asked, at which step, and what went wrong. The engine's fallback text is never shown as if it were a real answer.

Principle 02

Engine internals stay visible

Latency, token counts, retries, cost and the raw trace are available on every run from the Telemetry tab, without a debug flag or a separate build.

Principle 03

Both sides get the same weight

The pro and con columns are identical in width, type size and treatment, so the layout can't suggest a winner before the judge has scored the run.

ProStrongest arguments for
  • Tier-one volume is genuinely repetitive. Password, shipping and plan-change tickets are 45–60% of the queue at this size, so the case doesn't depend on the model being clever.
  • The knowledge base is an asset either way. Documentation counted here as an adoption cost is a cost the company already pays in hiring ramp.
  • On routine tickets, speed matters more than precision. The real comparison is against a multi-hour queue, not against an expert.
  • Rollback is cheap. Annual contracts, no data migration, no reorganisation. A failed pilot costs one quarter.
ConStrongest arguments against
  • Vendor deflection figures assume a mature knowledge base. Applied to a company without one, year-one economics likely invert.
  • The 3% of tickets that touch money carry all the downside. A wrong refund answer is a chargeback or a disclosure incident, not a bad satisfaction score.
  • Correct routing is the least reliable part on day one, and every optimistic scenario assumes it works.
  • Support tickets are the clearest product signal the company has. Deflecting them also deflects the feedback that tells you what to build.
Unresolved after four rounds
  • Whether deflection holds above 30% without a dedicated content owner.
  • Accessibility effects for non-native speakers, cited in both directions with no clean evidence either way.
  • Whether losing raw ticket text measurably degrades product prioritisation.
What would change this verdict
  • A measured month-one deflection rate below 15% at comparable company size.
  • Evidence that category-based routing fails on more than 1% of money-touching tickets.
  • Vendor pricing shifting from per-seat to per-resolution, which reverses the labour math.

Mode concept · not yet in core

Consensus

Agents answer independently, then see each other's positions and either revise or defend holding. The result reports where they landed, who held out, and whether the movement was reasoned.

Round 1 — Blind

Seven independent answers

No agent sees another. Positions and confidence are recorded before any influence.

Round 2 — Exposed

Revise or hold

Each agent sees the spread and must either move or give a reason for holding.

Round 3 — Settle

Final positions

Reports the band they settled into, the holdouts, and whether movement looked reasoned or like herding.

Convergence plotthe signature visual for this mode
Why this reuses the debate shell

The run header, stepper, inspector rail, evidence ledger and telemetry are all mode-agnostic. A new mode supplies a stage sequence, a stream renderer and a result renderer, and inherits the rest.

Mode concept · not yet in core

Advisory Panel

Each advisor works from a named perspective and answers in its own voice. Nobody argues against anyone else and no winner is declared — a chair reads the advice and writes up where it agrees and where it doesn't.

Design system

Tokens and rules

Serif type is reserved for the system's conclusions — resolutions, verdict headlines, section titles. Sans handles everything you read at length or click on. Monospace marks any value the engine measured. Colour is used only to distinguish pro, con and neutral.

Rules the system enforces

Pro and con columns are symmetrical

The two columns share a width, a type size and a background. When one side produces more text, its column scrolls rather than growing.

Serif type is used only for conclusions

Display type appears on resolutions, verdict headlines and section titles. Never on interface chrome, and never on an agent's turn.

Engine-measured numbers use monospace

Latency, tokens, cost and confidence are set in monospace so they read as measurements rather than as numbers written into prose.

Every section has a failed state

Each section is designed for empty, loading and failed. The failed state names both the model that was asked and why it didn't return anything usable.
